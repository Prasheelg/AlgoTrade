NR7 Breakout Playbook

Wait for the quietest day of the last seven. Trade the break of that day's range the next morning, risk the size of the range, and aim for three times it.

Bottom line.

Backtest results on index data, after costs. Not a promise of future returns and not financial advice. Paper-trade it before risking money.

1 · The idea

Quiet days are followed by moves

Markets alternate between calm and movement. Toby Crabel, a futures trader and fund manager, showed in his 1990 book Day Trading with Short Term Price Patterns and Opening Range Breakout that a day with an unusually small range is often followed by a larger move. NR7 means "narrowest range of 7 days". You don't predict the direction. You let the market pick it, then follow.

How an NR7 trade looks

The highlighted candle has the smallest high-to-low range of the last seven. Its high becomes the buy trigger and its low the sell trigger for the next day only. In this drawing price breaks above, so the trade is long, with the stop at the NR7 low and the target three ranges above the entry.

The rules

  1. Find an NR7 day.After the close, check whether today's range (high minus low) is the smallest of the last 7 trading days, today included.
  2. Set two triggers for tomorrow.Buy if price trades above today's high. Sell short if it trades below today's low. The first one hit is your trade.
  3. Put the stop at the other end.Long: stop at the NR7 low. Short: stop at the NR7 high. Your risk, called 1R, is the NR7 range.
  4. Aim for 3R.Target is three times the risk from your entry. If it isn't reached, close the trade at the end of the 5th trading day (entry day counts as day 1).
  5. One shot only.If neither trigger is hit tomorrow, cancel both. If you are stopped out, you are done with that signal: don't reverse. Hold one trade per index at a time.

3 · Risk and reward

Lose small often, win big sometimes

Everything here is measured in R, the amount you risk on a trade. If the NR7 range is 155 Nifty points, 1R is 155 points, which is on one Nifty futures lot of 65 units. A +3R winner then makes about three times that, and a stop-out loses about one R.

What happens to 100 Nifty trades

Result of every trade

Most trades end near −1R (the stop) or near +3R (the target). The middle is trades closed on day 5. A few land below −1R when the market gaps past the stop overnight.

Why a 37% win rate is enough

With winners averaging about 2.3R and losers about 1R, you break even if roughly 31% of trades win. NR7 has won about 37% of the time since 2000. That gap is the whole edge, so it is thin: expect long runs where it disappears.

5 · Trade plan

A day in the life of one trade

  1. 15:30Market closes. Wait for the final daily candle (official close, high and low).
  2. 15:45–20:00Run the scan. If today is an NR7 day, write down the buy level (today's high), sell level (today's low), risk in points, and both targets. Check the event calendar. Work out position size.
  3. Next day 09:15Watch the spot index. The moment it trades above the high or below the low, enter in that direction. A gap open beyond a level means you enter at the open.
  4. Right after fillPlace the stop and the target. Cancel the other trigger. If nothing triggers by 15:30, cancel everything.
  5. Days 2–5Leave it alone. The stop or target closes it, or you close it near the close (about 15:15) on day 5.
  6. After exitLog the trade: date, side, entry, exit, R, slippage versus the planned level.

When not to take a new trade

  • Union Budget day and the day before. Also general election results and exit-poll days. Gaps on these days can blow past a stop.
  • Short special sessions. Muhurat trading or weekend test sessions create fake narrow days. Ignore them as NR7 days.
  • Already in a trade on that index. One position per index.
  • Near futures expiry. If fewer than 5 trading days remain on the current futures contract, use next month's.
  • Overnight shock news such as war or a major global crash. This is a judgment call, not a tested rule.

Filters that did not help

  • Skipping gap opens. Trades that opened beyond the trigger did about as well as the rest.
  • Skipping very tiny NR7 days. No consistent difference across indices.
  • Day of the week. The pattern changed from index to index, which points to noise.

One filter that did help a little

Position size

Risk a fixed 0.5–1% of your trading capital per trade. Lots = (capital × risk %) ÷ (risk points × lot size), rounded down. Example: ₹10 lakh capital at 1% is ₹10,000 per trade. With a 155-point Nifty NR7 range, that is ₹10,075 per lot, so one lot. If the range is twice as wide, skip the trade or use options to keep the rupee risk the same.

The worst drawdown since 2000 was about 25R on Nifty. At 1% risk per trade that is roughly a 25% fall in the account. At 0.5% it is about 13%. Pick the size you can live through for years.

Futures, options, or both

7 · Coding it

Yes, this is easy to automate

NR7 needs only daily high and low. The scan is a few lines, and the order logic is a standard stop-entry with a stop-loss, a target and a time exit. It is the simplest strategy of everything tested in this project.

Evening scan

import math
import pandas as pd

LOT = {"NIFTY": 65, "BANKNIFTY": 30, "SENSEX": 20}   # check your broker's contract list

def nr7_signal(daily: pd.DataFrame, index: str, capital: float, risk_pct: float = 0.01):
    """daily: completed daily candles with open, high, low, close; last row is today."""
    rng = daily["high"] - daily["low"]
    if rng.iloc[-1] > rng.iloc[-7:].min():          # not the narrowest of the last 7 days
        return None
    hi, lo = daily["high"].iloc[-1], daily["low"].iloc[-1]
    risk = hi - lo
    lots = math.floor(capital * risk_pct / (risk * LOT[index]))
    return {
        "index": index, "lots": lots, "risk_pts": round(risk, 2),
        "buy_above": hi, "long_stop": lo, "long_target": hi + 3 * risk,
        "sell_below": lo, "short_stop": hi, "short_target": lo - 3 * risk,
        "valid_for": "next trading day only",
    }

Next-day execution, step by step

  1. Morning check.Load last night's signals. Skip any on an event day or with zero lots.
  2. Watch spot.Subscribe to the spot index on your broker's websocket. Trigger when spot crosses the buy or sell level, so live trading matches the backtest.
  3. Send the order.Buy or sell the future (or the chosen option) with a limit order a few ticks through the market. Several brokers block or convert plain market orders for API trading.
  4. Protect it.After the fill, place the stop-loss and target. When one fills, cancel the other. Most brokers lack a true one-cancels-the-other order in F&O, so your code does this.
  5. Time exit.On day 5 at about 15:15, close anything still open.
  6. Safety.A daily loss cap, a check that positions match what the code expects, and an alert if the data feed stops.

SEBI's retail algo rules (in force from April 2026) apply. Orders must come from a static IP registered with your broker, and you log in with 2FA every day. Staying under 10 orders per second keeps you out of separate algo registration. This strategy sends a handful of orders a week.

8 · Also know this

Things that will matter once you trade it

Expect long dry spells

Most trades lose

Backtest versus real fills

The test triggers on the spot index and fills exactly at the level, or at the open on a gap. Real futures trade at a premium or discount to spot and fill with slippage, especially at 09:15. Costs are included, and results stay positive even with costs doubled, but compare your real fills to the plan in your log.

Data limits

Nifty opening prices before 2011 are often recorded as the previous close, which flatters early gap fills slightly. Sensex has clean opens and shows the same early results. Bank Nifty's 2009 data has missing days, which produced one −9.7R loss that real trading would have cut at the stop. Sensex derivatives only became actively traded in recent years, so its long history describes how the index moves, not what you could have traded.

Don't over-tune it

NR10 looked a bit better than NR7 in the settings grid. Switching to whatever looked best in the past is how backtests stop matching real results. The standard NR7, 3R and 5 days were fixed before the 2000–2012 check and held up there.

Before real money

Paper-trade the first 10–20 signals, which takes about 4–8 months on one index. Then trade one lot and review every 20 trades against the numbers on this page. Taxes on F&O profits are treated as business income in India, so speak to a chartered accountant about your case.

9 · How it was tested

Method and assumptions

  • Data: daily index candles from Upstox's historical data API. Nifty 50 and Sensex from January 2000, Bank Nifty from June 2005, to . Diwali Muhurat and short weekend test sessions removed, and close-only records removed.
  • Entries: a stop order at the NR7 high or low on the next day only. If the day opens beyond the level, the fill is the open.
  • Conservative rules: if both levels trade on the entry day, the trade counts as stopped out. The target is never credited on the entry day, because the order of moves inside a day is unknown. A gap through the stop on a later day fills at that day's open.
  • Costs: round trip of about 0.017% of the index for Nifty, 0.022% for Bank Nifty and 0.015% for Sensex. That is today's brokerage, taxes and slippage for one lot (4, 12 and 12 index points at current levels). Also tested at double.
  • Not modelled: option prices, implied volatility, futures basis, and lot-size history. Results are in R, which is independent of lot size.
Backtests describe the past and can overstate what live trading gives. This page is technical information, not financial advice. Built from the backtest scripts in this project (swing.py and nr7_report.py).
